# Apartment fit and applications

## Your apartment profile

Open **Settings → Apartment requirements**. Paste your requirements or import a text/Markdown document, then edit the criteria. Mark must-haves, set preference weights, and use structured price, total-room, floor-area, or metro-line walking-time rules where appropriate. A bedroom is not the same as a total room; bedroom layout, kitchen size, furnishing, and lease duration should use semantic criteria supported by the listing's actual text.

On a listing, **Evaluate fit** runs deterministic checks and, when configured, Jev through the personal OpenRouter Decisions API. Every criterion is met, unmet, or unknown. The result shows a compact score summary and chips grouped into **Pros**, **Cons** and **Unverified**. Must-haves carry an asterisk; unknown criteria stay separate from confirmed cons. Unknowns are excluded from both sides of the score calculation. The weighted score is matched weight divided by known weight; coverage remains known weight divided by all requirement weights. An all-unknown assessment has no score, rather than zero. For example, four met, one unmet and five unknown equal-weight requirements score 8/10 with 50% coverage; failed must-haves remain separate, explicit disqualifiers. A failure does not erase the score. Fit sorting places assessments without confirmed must-have failures first, then failed assessments, with scores ordered within each group. Unknown must-haves retain their uncertainty warning and are not treated as confirmed failures. Existing scores are recalculated from saved verdicts without new model calls or changes to evidence and assessment dates. Changing the profile or listing makes older results stale.

Saved assessments appear beside each listing in both the list and grid views: a compact JEV score and a warning symbol when a must-have is failed, unresolved or out of date. Open the score disclosure to read evidence coverage and all warning labels. A listing without a saved assessment says **Not assessed**; an assessed listing with no known criteria says **No known criteria** and keeps its coverage and uncertainty warnings. Neither displays a zero. These summaries belong to the signed-in user, including on shared listings and for administrators; opening the list does not make model calls.

Scores display on a **0–10 scale**, with one decimal when needed (for example, a stored score of 71 displays as **7.1**, without a label or denominator). Red indicates a score below 5 or a failed must-have; amber indicates 5–7.9 or a stale assessment; green indicates 8–10 with no failed must-haves. Unknown must-haves stay visible but do not downgrade the score color. The detail assessment keeps coverage and warning chips visible. Repeated result cards fold those labels into the score disclosure. Stored scores and sorting remain on the original percentage scale; this display change does not rerun assessments.

Choose **JEV score** in **Sort by** to rank assessed listings without confirmed must-have failures first, then those with failures. Within each group, scores follow the selected direction. The Map uses the same order. Minimum fit filters still compare the numerical score; a score above the threshold does not waive a failed must-have. Sorting applies to all matching listings before pagination; unassessed listings stay last in either direction, after genuine zero scores. Equal scores use listing date, then listing ID for stable ordering. The sort is kept in the page URL. It does not reassess listings: stale scores retain their out-of-date warning, and must-have warnings remain visible.

The listing detail page shows the **Jev fit assessment** below the pictures on desktop and mobile. Criteria appear as keyword chips with topic icons; structured criteria retain their threshold. Click a chip or focus it and press Enter to read the complete requirement. Application tracking, notes and secondary enrichment sections start folded. Overview cards prioritize price, address, rooms, size and fit; detailed finance and market comparisons remain on the listing page. **Evaluate fit** assesses that listing on demand and reuses a fresh saved result; it keeps the previous result if the provider is unavailable. New listings remain unassessed until explicitly evaluated. Saving preferences alone does not start an assessment run.

In **Settings → Apartment requirements**, edit **JEV assessment instructions** to customize the guidance used by both manual and automatic assessments. Save with the profile; an empty value restores the default. Instructions are private to your account, limited to 4,000 characters, and changing them makes previous assessments out of date. Fixed evidence rules still apply: listing text cannot give instructions, missing facts remain unknown, and total rooms cannot establish bedrooms. Each criterion also supports optional **Chip keywords** (32 characters) and a **Chip icon**; automatic labels use the criterion's topic, not an invented shortened claim.

On the map, **Filters** starts closed at every width. Open it to change listing filters; expand **Map layers** for basemap/buildings/transit controls. **Pin legend** remains separately accessible while filters are closed. **Show all matches** reframes the current filtered pins if you zoom or pan away; it retains your filters. Cards continue to show exactly the filtered adverts within the map viewport, including shared-address stacks.

Metro-line criteria look for actual matching route membership in OpenStreetMap and calculate walking routes to candidate stops. They do not mistake the nearest generic stop for a U2 stop. Missing coordinates, incomplete map data, or unavailable routing produce unknown. These are evidence checks rather than guarantees of physical accessibility. Only text/Markdown profiles are supported; uploaded listing PDFs/photos remain attachments and are not OCR inputs for fit.

Semantic analysis requires a personal OpenRouter key. An administrator can set it in **Administration → System → OpenRouter (Jev)**. The password field is write-only: leave it blank to retain the saved key, enter a new value to replace it, or select **Remove saved key** and save to remove it. The saved key takes effect immediately and overrides `OPENROUTER_API_KEY_PERSONAL` from the deployment environment. Removing it restores that environment fallback, if present; the status indicates the active source. Fredy never uses the shared `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`.

The UI-saved key lives in the global SQLite settings alongside other instance secrets. It is not encrypted at rest; restrict access to the database and backups. No API response returns it. Without a key, the app explains the missing setup; numeric-only profiles remain usable. No claim of a live provider test is made by the installation tests.

## Applications, replies, visits and follow-ups

Open **Applications** to manage your application statuses directly in the list and see upcoming viewings and due follow-ups. Open a listing to edit its application, letter draft, dates and correspondence. Application state and correspondence are private to your account, including when the source listing is shared.

Track draft, submitted, replied, visit scheduled, follow-up, rejected, accepted, and withdrawn. Record notes and replies, set viewing/follow-up times, and download an ICS appointment. Status changes record what you have done outside Fredy, including applications sent through a property portal.

Letter drafts reuse Fredy's existing applicant profile and templates. Copy a letter to use on the portal or in your own email app; copying does not mark an application submitted. No email connection is required. Fredy does not send or schedule application emails or synchronize a mailbox. Portal submission is available only through explicitly enabled browser auto-apply rules described below.

Existing applications and correspondence are preserved. Historical mail tables and columns remain for database compatibility, but the email integration and its background jobs have been removed. Existing scheduled sends are inactive and cannot be rescheduled. Listing notification channels are separate and continue to work.

Automatic deployment and recovery instructions: [Mac Mini deployment](../tools/deploy/README.md).

## Search area and location review

A job's saved drawn area applies to every source in that job. An ImmoScout source configured with `exclusioncriteria=swapflat` also excludes swap offers from all its sources, independently of the portal's own filtering. Search eligibility is stored separately from activity, user-hidden listings and fit scores: rechecking never deletes notes, applications or attachments. Saving the job or changing a listing's location/details rechecks eligibility against the current boundary.

The default list, map, MCP results, dashboard counts and notifications contain matching listings only. **Location review** opens uncertain locations separately; **Filters → Excluded by search** shows confirmed outside-area and swap offers. Open a review listing and correct its address/map position to resolve it; it moves to matching or excluded immediately. A known swap is always excluded. Legacy coordinates, missing points, withheld streets and neighborhood-center geocodes remain uncertain until precise evidence is available.

ImmoScout advert street coordinates are retained. Geocoding preserves precision evidence through the cache; only house-level results qualify for area decisions. Rechecks keep manually hidden rows hidden and never restore old automatic/user deletions whose origin cannot be distinguished.

U-Bahn walking checks discover stops with an explicit `SUBWAY` mode from the configured Transitous service and batch walking routes to up to 200 nearby platforms in groups of 50. A complete local search with no subway platforms expands in stages up to ten kilometres to obtain actual walking-route evidence for outer districts. The ten-minute cutoff is compared against exact route seconds, not rounded minutes. Named lines use the configured Overpass endpoint. A complete empty local search covers even a generous three metres per second walking bound; routes to verified farther platforms provide the supporting walking evidence. Approximate listing locations and incomplete local station or route responses remain unknown; a neighborhood center cannot establish a precise walking time. Refreshing these checks can preserve the separate JEV semantic assessment.


## Browser-driven automatic applications

**Application settings → Automatic applications** configures a private browser connection, browser-agent model, applicant documents, and per-search rules. Rules start disabled. Enabling a rule authorizes paid assessments and browser inference plus automatic submission on its selected portals, without approving every application individually. Configure the personal OpenRouter key in Administration, select a browser model separately from JEV, and use **Check model** before enabling rules.

Pair one isolated browser connection per portal/account. Its token is shown once: install and run the worker using [the worker guide](../tools/auto-apply/README.md). Server mode runs alongside Fredy; desktop mode runs the same worker on your computer. Open the browser from Fredy, sign into the portal directly, and verify the visible account identity. The worker restarts that profile and checks that the identity persists. Password and two-factor screens are handled by you with the agent stopped. Browser sessions are separate from scraper sessions and from your unrelated tabs. Portal login verification does not prove a completed application flow: unknown form/request shapes pause for intervention, and live portal acceptance must be qualified separately.

Rules belong to the search owner and select ImmoScout24, Immowelt, and/or Kleinanzeigen, a minimum fit (8/10 initially, configurable in tenths), and a browser with a verified login for each portal. The rule applies only to new listings discovered or manually added after activation, including assessments delayed by an outage. Existing inventory and price changes are not backfilled. Unknown criteria do not reduce the known-only score or prevent application. All-unknown results have no qualifying score; confirmed must-have failures block submission. Unresolved locations are allowed, but confirmed outside-area listings, invalid areas, known search exclusions, inactive/hidden adverts, and scam verdicts are blocked.

The three portal adapters are currently tested against local representative forms. Live submission compatibility is not yet verified; authenticated qualification must confirm the actual portal form, upload and submission contracts before claiming support.

Applicant documents are private to your account, stored in the database, and separate from exposés attached to listings. Upload PDF, JPEG or PNG files up to 10 MB each (20 files and 100 MB total). Add an explanation of what each file contains and when you want to share it; these optional notes can be edited later, up to 2,000 characters. Explanations appear beside documents when selecting them for a search and remain internal notes, never appended to the application message. Editing a note preserves the uploaded bytes, content hash, and past receipts. Existing files keep working with empty explanations until you add them.

Select at most ten documents per rule. All selected files are attached when the portal supports attachments; unsupported types, failed uploads, or insufficient attachment slots pause the run. When attachments are entirely unavailable, the message may be sent and the receipt records that omission. Deleting a selected file prevents pending applications from uploading it. Temporary worker copies are not retained.

Applications and listing details show progress, exact sent content and document hashes, measured model usage, confirmation evidence, and intervention controls. Messages use your saved draft when present, otherwise the existing profile/template letter. The agent may select saved facts for fields, but cannot invent mandatory answers. **Open browser** pauses agent control; **Resume** releases the browser back to automation. Missing facts must be saved to your applicant profile before resuming the application.

A portal confirmation, not an agent success statement, marks an application submitted. A timeout or crash after submission intent becomes **uncertain**. Inspect the portal's sent-message history and use **Reconcile** to record whether it was sent; uncertainty is never automatically retried. A negative reconciliation cancels the attempt and retains its duplicate-prevention receipt. Ordinary failures before any possible send may retry twice; human-input cases require intervention. Duplicate protection spans searches and retained portal receipts, including deleted listings.

Browser views, frames, documents and receipts are owner-only. Login frames/keystrokes remain ephemeral; application records and applicant files are included in database backups. Keep server profiles under the persisted conf volume as described in the worker guide. Deployment drains the worker and blocks snapshots while submissions remain uncertain or browser profiles have not acknowledged closure. No public browser/CDP endpoint is required.
